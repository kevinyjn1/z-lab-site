1Research

The Z Lab's research can be summarized into two words: Matter and Machine.

1.1 Matter 1.2 Machine 1.3 At a glance

In the Matter domain, we synergistically integrate statistical physics, molecular simulations, artificial intelligence, and neutron scattering experiments to extend our understanding of rare/extreme events and long timescale phenomena in complex materials. Particular emphasis is given to the physics and chemistry of liquids, glasses, and complex fluids, especially at interfaces, under extreme conditions, or when driven away from equilibrium.

Concurrently, on the Machine front, we build robots for extreme environments, including swarm robots, wheel-leg hybrid robots, and soft robots.

These two research areas, spanning from fundamental to applied, serve as integral pillars in the lab's overarching mission to foster a sustainable, resilient, and secure energy infrastructure.

Liquids, glasses, and complex fluids
Physics and chemistry of liquids, glasses, and complex fluids, especially under interfacial, extreme, and non-equilibrium conditions: water, metallic liquids, molten salts, ionic liquids, electrolyte solutions.

Non-equilibrium statistical mechanics, molecular fluid mechanics, accelerated molecular simulations, and understandable AI methods. See the lab's software.
Neutron scattering
Neutron scattering, sources, and instrumentation, including the EXPANSE and CHESS spectrometers for the Second Target Station of the Spallation Neutron Source.
Rare events and long timescales
Rare/extreme events and long timescale phenomena in complex materials.

The water that never froze

Ordinary water freezes long before it can be cooled far enough to test a prediction from simulations: that below its famous density maximum, liquid water's density should stop falling and turn upward again. Confining heavy water in silica pores only 15 Å wide keeps it liquid down to 160 K, and a neutron diffraction peak then reads out its density. It passes through a minimum at 210 K, the first experimental report of one (Liu et al., Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. U.S.A. 2007).

Ordinary water in the same pores does the same at 200 K (Liu et al., J. Chem. Phys. 2013), while in water-repelling carbon pores the minimum is absent (Zhang et al., J. Phys. Chem. B 2009; Liu et al., J. Chem. Phys. 2015).

1.2Machine

Robots for extreme environments, including swarm robots, wheel-leg hybrid robots, and soft robots.

Robots in extreme environments
Robots for mission-critical work in extreme environments. The near-term focus is the energy sector and homeland security.
Soft robots and human-compatible machines
An untethered soft robotic arm, and Kraken, a MOOSE-based multiphysics simulation and control platform for soft robots.
Swarm robots and collective intelligence
Many simple robots that coordinate to complete tasks no single robot could handle. Atombot Hydrogen builds a miniature swarm platform for studying emergent behavior in many-robot systems.
Wheel-leg hybrid robots
Atombot Helium works toward a more practical robot unit: an advanced wheel-legged robot.

Atombot

Atombot is the lab's student robotics team: about 50 undergraduate, master's, and PhD students, working with the Multidisciplinary Design Program. It runs two projects, Hydrogen and Helium, and building hardware is the main focus.
