Matter in Extremes, Machine for Extremes

The Z Lab's research can be summarized into two words: Matter and Machine. In the Matter domain, we synergistically integrate statistical physics, molecular simulations, artificial intelligence, and neutron scattering experiments to extend our understanding of rare/extreme events and long timescale phenomena in complex materials. Particular emphasis is given to the physics and chemistry of liquids, glasses, and complex fluids, especially at interfaces, under extreme conditions, or when driven away from equilibrium. Concurrently, on the Machine front, we build robots for extreme environments, including swarm robots, wheel-leg hybrid robots, and soft robots. These two research areas, spanning from fundamental to applied, serve as integral pillars in their overarching mission to foster a sustainable, resilient, and secure energy infrastructure.

1.1Matter

1.1.1Physics and chemistry of liquids, glasses, and complex fluids, especially under interfacial/extreme/non-equilibrium conditions (water, metallic liquids, molten salts, ionic liquids, electrolyte solutions)

The water that never froze

Held in pores 15 Å wide, water stays liquid down to 160 K, and its density passes through a minimum at 210 K, the first seen in an experiment (Liu et al., Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. U.S.A. 2007).

1.1.2Non-equilibrium statistical mechanics, molecular fluid mechanics, accelerated molecular simulations, understandable AI methods

From 100 atoms to 100,000

Neural networks trained on first-principles calculations of about 100 atoms carry that accuracy over to simulations of 100,000 (Lee et al., J. Phys. Chem. B 2021; Grizzi et al., Phys. Rev. Mater. 2026).

1.1.3Neutron scattering, sources, and instrumentation

Two clocks in a glass-forming salt

Neutrons time how a ripple in a molten salt's density fades: in two steps, the slow one lengthening from 17 picoseconds to 6 nanoseconds on cooling (Luo et al., Nature Commun. 2022).

1.1.4Rare/extreme events and long timescale phenomena in complex materials

Flood the landscape

Filling an energy landscape from the bottom up records the height at which each valley joins the next: a full account of the barriers behind rare events (Mirth et al., J. Chem. Phys. 2021).

1.2Machine

1.2.1Robots in extreme environments

Steer the underwater arm

A water-driven soft arm with only eight on-off valves: the lab's automatically tuned controller ended inside 39 of 40 goals (Null et al., IEEE Robot. Autom. Lett. 2024).

1.2.2Swarm robots and collective intelligence

Lose two, keep going

Many small, cheap robots that carry on when a few of them fail (Michigan Robotics seminar, 2025).

1.2.3Wheel-leg hybrid robots

Wheels and legs in one machine

The lab works with a Unitree Go2-W, a quadruped with a driven wheel on every leg: it rolls where it can and jumps where it must.

1.2.4Soft robots and human-compatible machines

A simulator for soft machines

Kraken, the lab's open simulator for soft materials and robots, reproduces the bounce of silicone bellows to within about 3% (Wandke and Z, IEEE Access 2023).

Atombot

The lab's student robotics team: about 50 students building swarm and wheel-legged robots with the Multidisciplinary Design Program.
